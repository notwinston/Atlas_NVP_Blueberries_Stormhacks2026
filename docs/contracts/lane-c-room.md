## 1 · The room frame

README F4, restated. Once a RoomModel is active, Unity world space **is** the room frame.

```text
   seen from above                       +Z   along Df, the long side of the free rectangle
                                          ^
        outline (wall to wall) ___________|____________________
                              |           |                    |
                              |   ........|.................   |
                              |   :       |   free rect    :   |    free rectangle Wf x Df:
                              |   :   ....|.........       :   |    0.30 m inside walls and furniture
                              |   :   :   |        :       :   |
                              |   :   :   O--------:-------:---|----> +X   along Wf, the short side
                              |   :   :  lap square:       :   |
                              |   :   :............:       :   |    lap square s x s:
                              |   :........................:   |    at least 0.20 m inside the free rectangle
                              |________________________________|

   O = the anchor = world origin, on the floor.   +Y is up, out of the page.   y = 0 is the floor.
```

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Origin | Centre of the free rectangle, on the floor. The ARAnchor sits there |
| Axes | `+X` along `Wf`, `+Z` along `Df`, `+Y` up. Left-handed (Unity). `Wf ≤ Df` always (decided here): X is the short side |
| Sense of X | Of the two frames that satisfy the row above, the one whose yaw in the AR session frame lies in (−90°, +90°] (decided here) |
| Units | Metres. Radians in code and in files. Degrees only in UI text |
| Floor point | `float2 (x, z)`: `.x` is x, `.y` is **z** |
| Yaw ψ | Rotation about `+Y`, Unity's sense: positive turns `+Z` towards `+X` (clockwise seen from above). It carries local `+X` to (cos ψ, −sin ψ) and local `+Z` to (sin ψ, cos ψ) in (x, z). `quaternion.RotateY(ψ)` |
| Polygon winding | Counter-clockwise when drawn with `+X` to the right and `+Z` up the page (the view from above): signed area ½ Σ (xᵢ·zᵢ₊₁ − xᵢ₊₁·zᵢ) > 0. Holes are clockwise. A ring is never closed by repeating its first point |
| Head | The world pose of `Atlas Head` (`AtlasBootContext.Head`, lane A §9.2): the midpoint between the eyes. It equals the tracked camera until `IStereoRig.HeadOffset` is measured. A depth image is taken from the tracked camera itself (§7.2) |
| Before a room is active | World space is the AR session frame; `y = 0` is wherever the session started |

How world space becomes the room frame: every frame, in step 1, the pose pipeline moves the XR Origin's transform
(`AtlasBootContext.XrOrigin`) so that the anchor's world pose is the identity (yaw only; gravity stays up), with
the method of AF §5.3; the maths is `Atlas.Scan.RoomFrame`. The AR session frame is the local frame of the
tracked camera's parent, where trackables sit. Everything lane
C stores is relative to the anchor, so room content, Bump Guard and every flat space use coordinates as they are
(D16). In the Editor with a pose log or the keyboard walker there is no anchor: the source delivers room-frame
poses and the origin stays at the identity.

## 2 · The RoomModel

One RoomModel describes one scanned room. Every space reads it; no space holds a room size of its own (§4.1).
It exists as a JSON file (§2.1 to §2.4), as C# types (§2.5) and as a schema
([`roommodel.schema.json`](roommodel.schema.json), JSON Schema draft 2020-12).

### 2.1 Fields

### 2.2 The distance field `sdf`

| Key | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `cell` | number | Always `0.05` (m). The grid axes are the room's X and Z |
| `width`, `height` | integers, 12 to 400 | Cells along X and along Z |
| `originX`, `originZ` | numbers, m | Room coordinates of the **low corner of cell (0, 0)**. Multiples of 0.025: the origin of the room frame lies on a cell corner when the free rectangle has an even number of cells along that axis, and on a cell centre when it has an odd number |
| `data` | string | `width × height` signed 16-bit **little-endian** integers, **millimetres**, base64 (standard alphabet, with padding), no line breaks |

- **Layout.** Cell (i, j) covers x ∈ [`originX` + 0.05·i, `originX` + 0.05·(i + 1)) and z likewise with j. The
  stored value belongs to the cell **centre** (`originX` + 0.05·(i + ½), `originZ` + 0.05·(j + ½)). Row-major, row
  0 at the lowest z: `index = j * width + i`.
- **Extent.** The grid covers the outline's bounding box and at least 0.25 m (5 cells) beyond it on every side.
  No cell on the rim of the grid is free. A 3 × 3 m room is 70 × 70 cells; the 8 × 8 m room of §7.4 is 170 × 170
  (57,800 bytes, 77 kB as base64).
- **Sign.** Positive in a free cell, negative in every other cell, never exactly 0.
- **Value.** In a free cell: the distance from its centre to the nearest not-free cell's **square** (so to the
  edge of FREE). In a not-free cell: minus the distance from its centre to the nearest free cell's square.
  Exact, not a chamfer approximation: `|d|² = min over rows j′ of ( h(i, j′)² + max(|j − j′| − ½, 0)² )·0.05²`,
  where `h(i, j′)` is the horizontal gap in cells from column i to the nearest target cell of row j′
  (`max(|i − i′| − ½, 0)`, ∞ when the row has none). Cells beyond the grid count as not-free.
- **Quantisation.** `mm = floor(metres · 1000 + 0.5)`, clamped to ±32,767. Reading divides by 1000. In memory the
  field is `float` metres.
- **The 0.05 m under-read.** FREE stops one cell short of every wall and obstacle (§2.8, rule G7). So the field
  reads **0.05 m less than the true distance** to a wall or obstacle whose face lies on a cell boundary (every
  synthetic room), and between 0.05 and 0.10 m less otherwise. At a wall face the field is −0.05 m. Bump Guard's
  thresholds are written for the field as stored (§8.3); only the G5 static-map check adds the 0.05 m back
  (§11.2 of the plan).
- **Sampling between centres** is bilinear (`RoomSdf.Sample`, §2.5). It is exact wherever the field is linear,
  which is everywhere near a straight wall. On a ridge of the field (the middle of the room) it reads up to
  0.035 m low, the safe side.

### 2.3 Regions and polygons

| Name | Definition | Stored as |
|---|---|---|
| FREE | Cells that are floor, inside the outline, and not within one cell of an obstacle or of the outline (§2.8) | The sign of `sdf` |
| WALK | The 4-connected part of {`sdf` > 0.25 m} that contains the origin. Every point of it is at least 0.30 m from every wall and obstacle: body half-width plus tracking error (§4.2) | `walkable` |
| Free rectangle | The best axis-aligned rectangle of WALK cells (§2.8, rule G10), `Wf × Df` | `freeRect` |
| Lap square | Side s, centred on the origin (§3) | `lapSquare` |

`walkable` is the boundary of the WALK cells traced along cell edges: every vertex is a cell corner, collinear
points are removed, nothing else is simplified (decided here). Each ring starts at its lowest point (lowest z,
then lowest x). Holes are ordered by their lowest point. The Home floor is meshed from `outline`, not from
`walkable`: the floor reaches the walls and runs under the furniture. `walkable` serves room fitting
(`Portal_Graph`: "every room cell inside the eroded free floor"), the Station view and floor markings.

### 2.4 Walls and objects

`walls[k]`:

| Key | Type, unit | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `a`, `b` | `[x, z]`, m | End points. The wall lies on an edge of `outline` and runs the same way, so the room is on its **left**. The fixtures have exactly one wall per outline edge, in outline order |
| `height` | number, m, 0.5 to 6 | Height above the floor. 2.4 when the scan did not see the ceiling (decided here) |
| `openings[]` | objects, at most 16 | `kind`: `"door"`, `"window"` or `"opening"`. `offset`: metres from `a` along the wall to the near edge. `width`: metres. `bottom`, `top`: metres above the floor, `bottom < top ≤ height`; a door has `bottom = 0`. Openings of one wall do not overlap |

An outline edge with no wall on it is an open side (for example a part of the room the walker left out). The
distance field treats every outline edge as a wall, with or without an opening (§2.8, rule G7).

`objects[k]`:

| Key | Type, unit | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | integer, 1 to 255 | Unique in the room. The link calls it `boxId`. A scan numbers objects 1, 2, … by decreasing volume, so id order is the Forge's queue order (decided here). Fixtures keep the ids they were given |
| `category` | string | One of the 23 names of §6 |
| `base` | `[x, y, z]`, m | **Centre of the box's base**, room frame. `y` is 0 for furniture on the floor and above 0 for a wall-mounted screen. This point is the origin of the object's mesh (GLB contract, §7.4) |
| `size` | `[w, h, d]`, m, each 0.02 to 6 | Full size: `w` along the box's local X, `h` up, `d` along its local Z |
| `yaw` | number, rad, (−π, π] | Rotation about `+Y` (§1) |
| `mesh` | string | What is drawn: `"placeholder"`, `"requested"`, `"ready"`, `"builtIn"`, or `"standIn"` (◆ `budget_room` only, §5.3) |
| `assetHash` | string | SHA-256 of the generated GLB, 64 lower-case hexadecimal digits, exactly when `mesh` is `"ready"`; otherwise empty. This is the `boxId → sha256` record of §7.4 |
| ◆ `triTarget` | integer | Triangle target sent with the Forge job: `clamp(60000 ÷ n, 3000, 5000)` with integer division, n being the number of objects that receive meshes (all of them, or the 20 largest by volume, ties to the lower id). `0` for an object that stays a placeholder box. 5000 up to twelve objects, 4615 at thirteen, 3000 at twenty (§4.5) |

Mesh states and who moves them:

| From | To | Who | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| (new) | `placeholder` | RoomModel builder | Scan confirmed |
| `placeholder` | `requested` | Asset client, through `IRoomContent.SetObjectState` | Upload accepted (202) |
| `requested` or `placeholder` | `ready` | Asset client, through `IRoomContent.SetObjectMesh` | Generated GLB validated and loaded |
| any but `ready` | `builtIn` | Asset client, through `IRoomContent.SetObjectMesh` | Fallback mesh placed (no photo, failed job, rejected asset, Forge unreachable) |
| `builtIn` | `ready` | Asset client | The Forge returned and the generated mesh arrived |

An object whose box does not reach into the band from 0.10 m to 2.0 m above the floor (a ceiling lamp, a rug) is
kept in `objects` but does not block the floor.

### 2.5 C# types


**Canonical text** (what `Write` produces and what the fixtures are):

- UTF-8 without a byte-order mark, line feeds, a final line feed.
- Keys in the order of the table in §2.1. One top-level key per line, indented two spaces. `walls` and
  `objects`: one element per line, indented four spaces; an empty list is `[]` on the key's line.
- Inside a value: `", "` after commas and `": "` after colons, no other white space.
- Integers (`version`, `width`, `height`, counts, `id`, `triTarget`, `route`) have no decimal point. Every other
  number is written in fixed notation with at most six decimals, trailing zeros removed, and always one decimal
  at least: `0.0`, `2.4`, `1.884526`, `-1.570796`. Never an exponent, never `-0.0`. Invariant culture.
- A file is at most 300 kB (§7.4).

**Geometry hash.** This is the "RoomModel hash" inside the link's `worldHash` (§7.3.1). It covers what the
Spectator must agree on to draw the room, and nothing that changes after the scan (not `mesh`, `assetHash`,
`triTarget`, `quality`, `anchor`, `id`, `createdUtc`). SHA-256 of these bytes, integers as 32-bit little-endian,
`mm(v) = floor(v · 1000 + 0.5)`:

```text
"ATLAS-ROOM-1"                                   12 ASCII bytes
sdf.width, sdf.height, mm(originX), mm(originZ)
the decoded bytes of sdf.data                    width * height * 2 bytes
mm(wf), mm(df)
outline count, then mm(x), mm(z) per point
wall count, then per wall: mm(a.x), mm(a.z), mm(b.x), mm(b.z), mm(height), opening count,
    then per opening: kind (0 door, 1 window, 2 opening), mm(offset), mm(width), mm(bottom), mm(top)
object count, then per object in file order: id, category value (section 6),
    mm(base.x), mm(base.y), mm(base.z), mm(size.w), mm(size.h), mm(size.d), floor(yaw * 10000 + 0.5)
```

The first 16 digits for each fixture are in §5.1; `validate_room.py` prints them.

### 2.7 Validation rules

`validate_room.py` applies the schema, then these rules. A file is valid when all hold. Tolerance on lengths is
1e-4 m unless stated.

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| Schema | Types, required keys, enumerations and ranges of `roommodel.schema.json`; no unknown key; file ≤ 300 kB |
| R1 | `outline` is a simple polygon, counter-clockwise, with no edge shorter than 0.05 m |
| R2 | `sdf.originX` and `originZ` are multiples of 0.025 m; the grid reaches at least 0.25 m beyond the outline on every side |
| R3 | `sdf.data` decodes to exactly `width × height × 2` bytes; no value is 0 |
| R4 | Every free cell (value > 0) has its centre inside the outline; no free cell on the rim of the grid |
| R5 | The field equals the exact distance transform of its own sign (§2.2) to within 1.5 mm |
| R6 | `wf ≤ df`; both are multiples of 0.05 m |
| R7 | The origin lies in WALK; every cell of the free rectangle is in WALK |
| R8 | The free rectangle is the best one under rule G10: no rectangle of WALK cells has a longer short side (counted up to 3.6 m), and none with that short side has a larger area |
| R9 | `walkable.outer` is counter-clockwise and every hole clockwise; every vertex is a cell corner; a cell centre is inside the polygon exactly when the cell is in WALK |
| R10 | `lapSquare.side` = clamp(`wf` − 0.4, 1.0, 3.2) |
| R11 | `l5` = s ÷ 1.0612750619050357, `l6` = s ÷ 1.3169578969248166, `r3` = 2s ÷ π. (Floors that differ from 1.4 and 1.2 give a warning, not an error) |
| R12 | `cells.glued` and `cells.aligned` equal the rules of §4 |
| R13 | `standing` = (`wf` < 1.5) |
| R14 | Every wall is at least 0.05 m long, lies on an outline edge to within 0.01 m and runs the same way |
| R15 | Every opening fits inside its wall, has `bottom < top ≤ height`, a door has `bottom = 0`, and openings of one wall do not overlap |
| R16 | Object ids are unique; `assetHash` is set exactly when `mesh` is `ready`; every base lies inside the grid and not more than 0.05 m below the floor |
| R17 | `triTarget` follows §2.4. A room with a `standIn` object has only `standIn` objects, each with `triTarget` 5000 and a stand-in mesh of exactly 5,000 triangles with no edge over 0.25 m (§9.5) |
| R18 | No cell overlapped by the footprint of an object that blocks the floor is free |
| R19 | (warning) A scanned room (`route` > 0) has a non-empty `anchor.id` |
| R20 | A file whose `id` names a G0 fixture has that fixture's reference values (§5.1); `budget_room` has twelve stand-ins totalling 60,000 triangles |

`RoomModelValidator` (§2.6) applies R1 to R19 to a room in memory. It leaves three checks to others: the refusal
of unknown keys (the C# reader ignores them), the stand-in mesh half of R17 (checked where the mesh is built,
`StandIn_Exact5000`), and R20 (the reference table is in `RoomModel_Fixtures`).

### 2.8 FloorScan grid rules

The engine-free core (`P1-C15`, `Atlas.Scan.Grid.FloorScanCore`) and `roommath.py` follow the same thirteen
rules, so they give the same cells. Plan §4.2 and R1 §C.3 give the outline; the points they leave open are
decided here.

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| G1 Axis | θ₀ = ¼·atan2(Σ wᵢ·sin 4αᵢ, Σ wᵢ·cos 4αᵢ), in (−45°, 45°]: αᵢ is the direction of wall i in the scan frame and wᵢ its length. Route 2 uses the `WallFace` planes (normal directions give the same θ₀); route 1 and synthetic rooms use the outline edges. \|θ₀\| < 1e-9 counts as 0. The work frame is the scan frame turned by θ₀ |
| G2 Grid | 5 cm cells. The lattice **starts at the low corner of the footprint's bounding box** in the work frame, so a room measured in whole cells gives the same cells wherever it lies in the session frame. The footprint is the outline; with no outline yet, the floor triangles and the walked path. Cells along an axis: `ceil(extent / 0.05 − 0.001) + 10`, which is 5 cells of margin on every side |
| G3 INSIDE | A cell is inside when its centre is inside the outline (even-odd rule). With no outline yet: every cell that is FLOOR or OBST |
| G4 FLOOR | Route 1 and synthetic rooms: every inside cell. Route 2: a cell under a `Floor` triangle that lies within 0.08 m of y0, or within 0.15 m of the walker's own path (its samples and the straight steps between them) |
| G5 OBST | A cell overlapped by a triangle that is not floor and has some part between 0.10 m and 2.0 m above y0 (route 2), or by the footprint of an object box that reaches into that band (all routes). Overlap is a separating-axis test; touching does not count: geometry nearer than 1e-4 m to a cell edge does not reach into the cell |
| G6 Pinholes | A 4-connected set of at most 4 inside cells that are neither FLOOR nor OBST, all of whose 4-neighbours are FLOOR, becomes FLOOR. Larger unseen areas stay not-free. Glass and mirrors are the reason (§4.2) |
| G7 FREE | FREE = FLOOR and not grow(OBST ∪ not INSIDE), where grow adds the eight neighbours of every cell (a 3 × 3 square). So FREE stops one cell short of every obstacle **and of every outline edge**, door openings included |
| G8 Field | The exact signed distance of §2.2 |
| G9 WALK | The 4-connected part of {field > 0.25 m} that contains the walker's cell when the sweep ends. If that cell is not in the set, the part whose nearest cell centre is closest to the walker |
| G10 Free rectangle | Among all axis-aligned rectangles of WALK cells choose by, in order: (1) the longest short side, counted only up to 3.6 m (72 cells), beyond which s gains nothing; (2) the largest area; (3) the centre nearest the walker; (4) the lowest row, then the lowest column. §3.2 calls the lap square "the largest square the walker can comfortably lap", so the short side comes before the area (decided here; see Open for the lead, item 4) |
| G11 Room frame | Origin at the rectangle's centre. X along its short side: a quarter turn is added when the rectangle is longer along the work frame's x, in the sense that keeps the frame's yaw in (−90°, 90°]. Cells are **re-indexed, never resampled**, which is why `originX` and `originZ` are multiples of 0.025 |
| G12 Polygon | `walkable` = the boundary of WALK traced along cell edges with the region on the left; where two cells touch only at a corner the trace turns left (§2.3) |
| G13 Objects | The boxes supplied (RoomPlan, route 3) come first. Then one box per 4-connected blob of at least 4 OBST cells inside the outline that are not only wall, window or door. A blob is dropped when at least half its cells lie under a supplied box. The RoomModel builder keeps the 64 largest by volume and numbers them by decreasing volume |

The grid core reports `FloorGridStatus.NoFloor` (nothing to build a grid from) or `NoWalkableFloor` (WALK is
empty). `ScanSolver` turns these into `ScanFailure.NoFloor` and `ScanFailure.TooSmall`; a free rectangle
with a side under 0.5 m (`RoomModelBuilder.MinFreeSide`) is `TooSmall` as well, and no RoomModel is produced.

## 3 · The loop-fit

Plan §4.4 and Appendix A.4. The scan never measures the room wall to wall; code always starts from the free
rectangle.

```text
s  = clamp( min(Wf, Df) − 0.4 , 1.0 , 3.2 )        metres     (= clamp(min(W, D) − 1.0, 1.0, 3.2) in an empty
                                                                rectangular room, where W = Wf + 0.6)
L5 = s / 1.0612750619050357                        q = 5      cosh(s / 2L) = √2 · cos(π/5); cosh(spacing) = φ
L6 = s / 1.3169578969248166                        q = 6      cosh(s / 2L) = √2 · cos(π/6); cosh(spacing) = 2
R3 = 2 s / π                                       q = 3      cos(s / 2R) = √2 · cos(π/3)
standing = min(Wf, Df) < 1.5
dial radius(q) = max(L5, floorL), max(L6, floorL), max(R3, floorR)      unless the walker opts into "strong"
```

- The two constants are the centre spacings of {4,5} and {4,6}, 2·asinh(√cos(2π/q)), in double precision. They
  are the same literals as on lane B's page. Using 1.0613 would give L = 1.884 m for the 3 × 3 m room where the
  plan's test wants 1.885 m.
- All arithmetic is `double`; results are stored as `float`.
- The lap square is centred on the origin with sides along X and Z. It lies at least 0.20 m inside the free
  rectangle, so a walker on it keeps 0.5 m from the nearest wall or scanned obstacle, whenever
  min(Wf, Df) ≥ 1.4 m. Below that s stays 1.0 m and the room is in standing mode anyway.
- At q = 4 no radius is needed; lane B's page fixes what the kernel receives.
- The comfort floors are constants in code until G2 measures them (open item 7). The RoomModel stores the values
  in force when it was built, and readers use the stored values.

## 4 · Cell grids

Both grids are cut from the free rectangle and centred on the origin. X carries the short side.

### 4.1 Glued cell (S1), plan §4.3

```text
nx = max(1, round(Wf / 1.5))      sizeX = Wf / nx
nz = max(1, round(Df / 1.5))      sizeZ = Df / nz             round(x) = floor(x + 0.5)
```

`round` is half-up, not the banker's rounding of `Math.Round`. Cells come out between 1.125 m and 2.25 m on a
side of at least 1.4 m. The `nx × nz` cells fill the free rectangle exactly; cell (ix, iz) has its low corner at
(−Wf/2 + ix·sizeX, −Df/2 + iz·sizeZ).

### 4.2 Aligned Cells grid (S3), plan §4.7

```text
n = max(3, ceil(Wf / 1.5))        g = Wf / n        m = floor(Df / g + 1e-6)
if g < 0.75:   n = 2,  g = Wf / 2,  m = 2          # corner demo only: five tiles round one post
```

`n` counts cells along X (the short side) and `m` along Z. The grid spans n·g = Wf along X and m·g ≤ Df along Z,
centred on the origin, so a strip of (Df − m·g)/2 stays outside it at each end. In the fallback the grid is
2 × 2 whatever Df is (decided here: the plan's comment says "a 2 × 2 grid"). The fallback applies whenever
Wf < 2.25 m.

### 7.2 `DepthFrame` and `IDepthSource`

```csharp
// File: ATLAS/Assets/Atlas/World/Pose/DepthFrame.cs
using Unity.Mathematics;

namespace Atlas.World
{
    /// <summary>
    /// One LiDAR depth image with the pose it was taken at. The buffer belongs to the depth source and is
    /// overwritten by its next read: use it within the frame, never keep it.
    /// </summary>
    public struct DepthFrame
    {
        public const int NominalWidth = 256;
        public const int NominalHeight = 192;
        public const float MinRange = 0.3f;     // metres: a nearer value is "no return"
        public const float MaxRange = 5.0f;     // metres: a farther value is "no return"

        /// the image. 0, NaN or a value outside [0.3, 5.0] means "no return".</summary>
        public float[] Depth;
        public int Width, Height;
        /// <summary>Pinhole intrinsics of this image, in its own pixels: fx, fy, cx, cy. cx, cy from the top-left corner.</summary>
        public float Fx, Fy, Cx, Cy;
        /// <summary>Pose of the tracked camera (not of the head) when the image was taken: same frame as HeadPose.Position.</summary>

        public quaternion Rotation;
        public double Time;                 // seconds, app clock

        public int Sequence;

        /// <summary>True when d is a return: finite and inside [0.3, 5.0] metres.</summary>
        public static bool IsReturn(float d);
        /// <summary>Camera-space point of pixel (u, v) at depth d: ((u + 0.5 - Cx) / Fx * d, -(v + 0.5 - Cy) / Fy * d, d).
        /// Camera space is Unity's: +X right, +Y up, +Z forward.</summary>
        public float3 Unproject(int u, int v, float d);
        /// <summary>Position + Rotation * Unproject(u, v, d).</summary>
        public float3 ToWorld(int u, int v, float d);
```

### 7.5 The pose pipeline: two streams

Frame-loop steps 1 and 3 (§6.3). One pose sample per frame feeds everything. The pipeline is lane A's
`ISenseStep` and `IPoseFilterStep` (lane A §7.1).

| Stream | Members | What it is | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw (safety) | `Raw`, `Sensors.Head`; `FrameContext.RawHeadPosition`, `RawHeadRotation`, `Tracking` | The pose as read, in the room frame. A relocalisation jump appears here at once | Bump Guard, the eye cameras, `State.headPos` and `headRot`, the pose log |
| Geometry | `Geometry`, `Step`, `PathFloor`, `JumpRejected`; `FrameContext.HeadPosition`, `HeadRotation`, `PoseJumpRejected` | The pose a space reads, and the floor displacement the curved worlds integrate. A jump is dropped from the **displacement**; the pose itself follows the raw pose, so a registered space stays registered (lane A §3) | The kernel step, every space |

Rules of the filter (`PoseFilter`, engine-free):

```text
d = Raw.Floor(t) − Raw.Floor(t − 1)                           # (x, z), metres; 0 on the first frame and after ResetGeometry
rejected = |d| > 0.20 m                                       # PoseFilter.JumpDistance (D8, section 4.6)
           or Raw.Tracking != Normal
           or d is not finite
Step       = rejected ? 0 : d
PathFloor += Step                                             # the head's floor point as the geometry has walked it
Geometry   = Raw                                              # position and rotation
JumpRejected = |d| > 0.20 m ;  if so JumpCount += 1 and JumpRejectedRaised(|d|)

notNormal += DeltaTime while Raw.Tracking != Normal, else notNormal = 0
TrackingLost becomes true when notNormal ≥ 0.5 s              # PoseFilter.TrackingLostSeconds; raises TrackingLostRaised once
TrackingLost becomes false on the first Normal frame          # raises TrackingRegained
```

- This is the same test, on the same displacement, that lane B's kernel applies to the `FloorDelta` it is handed
  (lane B §4.2). So `Step` equals the displacement the kernel integrates, frame for frame.
- A 0.5 m jump injected in replay is absent from the geometry stream (`Step` is zero on that frame and
  `PathFloor` does not contain it) and present in the raw stream (`P1-C11`). The kernel's walker frame does not
  move (`Safety_Independence`, pose-jump half).
- `ResetGeometry()` makes the next `d` zero, and that frame takes its `Raw.Floor` as the new `PathFloor`. The
  tracking-lost timer is not touched. It is the recentre entry point:
  lane A's `CommandRouter.Recentre` and SpaceLab call it together with `IFrameLoop.ResetWalkerToFloor`.
- `State.flags` bit 2 ("pose jump rejected") is `JumpRejected` of the frame; bits 0-1 are
  `TrackingStates.ToWire(Raw.Tracking)`.
- Bump Guard does not use this filter. It keeps its own jump rule on the raw stream (§8.4).

```csharp

using Unity.Mathematics;

{

    /// Reads the head once per frame and offers it as two streams (Master Plan sections 6.3 and 8.7).
    /// Published through AtlasServices. Its per-frame work is lane A's ISenseStep and IPoseFilterStep.
    /// </summary>
    public interface IPosePipeline
    {
        IPoseSource Source { get; set; }                 // may be replaced between frames
        IDepthSource Depth { get; set; }
        /// <summary>The active room, or null. Setting it puts positions in the room frame from the next frame
        /// and calls ResetGeometry.</summary>

        HeadPose Geometry { get; }                       // valid after step 3
        float2 Step { get; }                             // metres (x, z); zero when rejected
        float2 PathFloor { get; }                        // metres (x, z): Raw.Floor at the last reset plus every Step since
        bool JumpRejected { get; }                       // this frame
        int JumpCount { get; }                           // since start
        bool TrackingLost { get; }                       // not Normal for 0.5 s or more
        void ResetGeometry();

        /// <summary>Rotation prediction, milliseconds: 0 (off, the default) or 30 to 60 (section 8.7); the setter
        /// clamps to 0 .. 60. The rate is taken from the last two raw rotations (decided here: no extra sensor
        /// is read), and a predicted turn is never larger than 0.21 rad.</summary>
        float GyroPredictMs { get; set; }
        /// <summary>The rotation to add to the eyes this frame, in head space; identity when off. The
        /// composition root copies it to IStereoRig.PredictionOffset at step 6 (lane A section 9.6).</summary>
        quaternion PredictionRotation { get; }

        event Action<TrackingState> TrackingChanged;     // raised inside step 1
        event Action TrackingLostRaised;                 // raised inside step 3, once per loss
        event Action TrackingRegained;
        event Action<float> JumpRejectedRaised;          // metres
    }
```

Rules:

- The `TrackedPoseDriver` on the tracked camera runs on **Update only** (`updateType = Update`). The frame
  loop's `Update` runs after it (`[DefaultExecutionOrder(100)]`). Nothing else reads the head (F5).
- `Sense` and `Filter` allocate nothing.
- When no sample is available (`TryRead` false) the last pose is repeated with `Tracking = NotTracking`.
- Lane A's frame loop computes `FrameContext.FloorDelta` again from `HeadPosition` and hands it to the kernel
  with `Tracking == Normal`; because the kernel repeats the test above, the result is `Step`. The value
  `Filter` wrote is information only.
- A sample whose position is not a number gives a zero `Step` and never becomes the reference: the next good
  sample is measured from the last good one.
- Parameters declared by the composition root (`AppParams.DeclarePose`) and written to plain properties (F6),
  group `pose`: `pose.gyroPredict` (Bool, default off) and `pose.gyroLeadMs` (Float, 0 to 60 ms, default 40)
  together give `GyroPredictMs` (the lead when on, 0 when off); `pose.record` (Bool, default off) goes to
  `PoseLogRecorder.Enabled` (§7.6); `pose.source` (Enum `tracked`, `replay`, `walker`; default `tracked`),
  `replay.speed` (Float, 0 to 4, default 1) and `replay.loop` (Bool, default on) choose and steer the pose
  source through `Atlas.App.PoseSourceSwitch`.

### 7.6 The pose log: `walk_*.poselog`

A text file (decided here): one head pose per line, readable, diffable, easy to write from a test or a script.
`recordings/walk_<name>.poselog` in the repository (three synthetic walks in `sample_3x3`, described in
`recordings/README.md`); `Application.persistentDataPath/logs/walk_<yyyyMMdd_HHmmss>.poselog` (UTC) on the phone. About 75 bytes per sample: a five-minute walk at 60 Hz is 1.4 MB.

```text
ATLAS-POSELOG 1
room sample_3x3
frame room
created 2026-10-04T10:15:30Z
# any line that starts with '#' is a comment
data
0.000000 0.000000 1.600000 0.000000 0.000000 0.000000 0.000000 1.000000 2
0.016667 0.011667 1.600000 0.000000 0.000000 0.000000 0.000000 1.000000 2
```

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Encoding | UTF-8, no byte-order mark, line feeds. Invariant culture. Fields separated by single spaces |
| Line 1 | `ATLAS-POSELOG 1`. A reader rejects any other version |
| Header | `key value` lines until a line that is exactly `data`. `frame` is required: `room` or `session`. `room <id>` (the RoomModel the walk was recorded in), `created <ISO 8601 UTC>` and `device <text>` are optional. Unknown keys are ignored |
| Data line | Nine fields: `t x y z qx qy qz qw s` |
| `t` | Seconds since the first sample, six decimals, never decreasing. The first is `0.000000` |
| `x y z` | Head position, metres, six decimals, in the frame named in the header |
| `qx qy qz qw` | Head rotation, Unity quaternion order, six decimals |
| `s` | Tracking: `0` not tracking, `1` limited, `2` normal (the values of `TrackingState`) |
| Content | The **raw** stream. Depth is not recorded. A pose log is motion data of a person: it is recorded only when `pose.record` is on, and kept under the rules of §13.5 |

### 8.3 Inputs and the two layers

| Layer | Source | Storage |
|---|---|---|
| Static | `RoomModel.Sdf` | The room's own field, read with `RoomSdf.Sample` |
| Dynamic | Live depth | 5 cm cells on the same grid as the static field, held as dense arrays with a count per block of 8 × 8 cells, so a query where nothing is near costs nine counters: per cell the highest hit (metres above the floor), the time of the last hit, and a count of consecutive empty sightings |

```text
clear(p) = min( sdf.Sample(p) , distance from p to the nearest dynamic cell )
distance from p to a dynamic cell = max(0, |p − cell centre| − 0.025) ;  +∞ when no cell lies within 0.40 m
```

Dynamic layer, on every processed depth image, for every `DepthStride`-th pixel with a valid depth (rows and
columns 2, 6, 10, …), only while the map is trusted (a room is loaded, tracking `Normal`, anchor tracked):

```text
P = depth.ToWorld(u, v, d)                                    # room frame
stamp:  if 0.10 < P.y < 2.0 and P is more than 0.30 m nearer than the static map predicts along its ray:
            the ray, carried on 0.30 m from P, reaches neither the floor nor a cell with sdf <= 0,
            and sdf.Sample(P.xz) > 0
        then
            cell(P.xz).height = max(cell.height, P.y) ;  cell.lastHit = now ;  cell.empty = 0
clear:  for every dynamic cell the ray from the camera to P passes over (floor projection, 0.05 m steps),
        at a floor distance at least 0.10 m short of P:
            if the ray's height over that cell is below cell.height − 0.05:  mark the cell "seen empty" in this image
after the image:
        a cell seen empty in this image and not hit in it:  cell.empty += 1 ;  cleared when cell.empty ≥ 3
        a cell neither hit nor seen empty:                  unchanged (leaving the image does not clear it)
        a cell with now − cell.lastHit > 30 s:              ignored by clear() at once, removed by a sweep of
                                                            8 blocks per step
```

The stamp rule is the plan's "more than 0.3 m nearer than the static map predicts", taken along the ray with the
floor as part of the map; it also catches an object close beside a wall, which a test of `sdf.Sample(P.xz)`
alone would miss.

Depth sectors, on the same samples, whether or not the map is trusted. With w = `Depth.Rotation` · (camera-space
point), the offset of the hit from the camera in the frame's axes: its **floor range** is |w.xz|, its height
above the floor is `Depth.Position.y` + w.y, and its azimuth is the signed angle from the camera's forward axis
projected on the floor to w.xz, positive to the right (looking straight up or down, the image's up axis stands
in for forward). Sector k (0 to 6)
covers azimuths [−35° + 10°·k, −25° + 10°·k); sector 3 is straight ahead. Each sector keeps the smallest floor
range among its samples whose height is between 0.10 m and 2.0 m. A sector with no such sample reads +∞. The
sectors are the whole of Bump Guard while tracking is not Normal (§8.5): ARKit still delivers the rotation and
the last height then, so levelling keeps working.

### 8.4 Velocity and the pose jump

```text
on every Tick with Head.Tracking == Normal, with p = Head.Floor (otherwise the last speed stands):
    if |p − previous p| > 0.20 m in one frame:    ring cleared                    # jump: position taken at once
    push (Time, p) into a ring of 16 samples
    v = (p − p_old) / (Time − t_old)   with p_old the oldest sample not older than 0.2 s ;  v = 0 with one sample
    speed = min(|v|, 2.0)
```

A relocalisation therefore never produces a look-ahead spike: the frame after a jump has zero velocity, and the
new position is used for `clear(head)` at once. This is the pose-jump half of `Safety_Independence`.

### 8.5 Level logic

```text
Tick(sensors):                                                 # first call of the frame
  if not RoomActive:  the static map is put away ;  it is loaded again when RoomActive returns
  process depth if HasDepth (8.3) ; update velocity (8.4)
  notNormal = (Head.Tracking != Normal or not AnchorTracked) ? Time − time of the first such frame : 0

  if a room is loaded and notNormal == 0:                      # the static map is trusted
      now       = clear(head)
      path(T)   = min of clear() at head + v·t for t = 0 .. T in steps of 0.05 m of travel (both ends included)
      direction = towards the hazard (8.6)
  else:                                                        # live depth alone, in the head frame
      now       = smallest of the seven sector ranges          # +∞ without depth, or when the image is over 0.25 s old
      path(T)   = now − lastSpeed · T                          # lastSpeed: the speed of the last trusted frame
      direction = centre azimuth a of that sector: (sin a, cos a)

  want  = STRONG if now < 0.20 or path(0.4 s) < 0.05
          WARN   if now < 0.30 or path(0.8 s) < 0.05
          CLEAR  otherwise
  floor = STRONG if now < 0.25 or path(0.4 s) < 0.10           # the same tests with RelaxDistance added
          WARN   if now < 0.35 or path(0.8 s) < 0.10
          CLEAR  otherwise

  if notNormal ≥ 0.5 s:        want = floor = TRACKING_LOST

  if want > level:             level = want ;  relax = 0       # a rise is immediate: exactly one rising edge
  else if floor ≥ level:       relax = 0                       # still inside the margin
  else:  relax = time since the first such frame ;  if relax ≥ 0.3 s:  level = floor ;  relax = 0
                                                               # TRACKING_LOST falls the same way: 0.3 s after
                                                               # tracking is Normal again
  rescan test (8.7), with each processed depth image
  when Armed (8.8):  BumpCue.Fire(level, direction) ;  BumpCue.Rescan(RescanAdvised)
  otherwise:         BumpCue.Fire(CLEAR, 0) ;  BumpCue.Rescan(false)          # an operator cue still shows
  always:            BumpCue.Advance(Time, DeltaTime)
```

Hysteresis in one sentence: a level rises at once and falls only after the stricter test (thresholds plus
0.05 m) has passed without a break for 0.3 s; it may then fall by more than one step.

With no active room (before the first scan is confirmed) the head-frame branch runs, so live depth still warns.

Worked numbers (§4.10; the static field reads 0.05 m short, so "from the wall" = clearance + 0.05):

| Walking straight at a wall at | WARN starts, from the wall | STRONG starts, from the wall | Lead time of WARN to predicted contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 m/s | 0.35 m (`now` rule) | 0.25 m (`now` rule) | 1.00 s |
| 0.5 m/s | 0.50 m | 0.30 m | 0.90 s |
| 0.7 m/s | 0.66 m | 0.38 m | 0.87 s |
| 1.0 m/s | 0.90 m | 0.50 m | 0.85 s |

Path rule: WARN when clearance < 0.05 + 0.8·v, STRONG when clearance < 0.05 + 0.4·v. Predicted contact is where
the path reaches `clear() = 0`, 5 cm before the wall. Walking along a wall at 0.5 m (clearance 0.45 m) raises
nothing. At a lap corner the wall ahead is 0.5 m away: a WARN there needs more than 0.5 m/s (open item 19).

### 8.6 Direction

| Case | World direction | 
|---|---|
| The smallest `clear()` is at the head, static field smaller | −`sdf.Gradient(head)` |
| The smallest `clear()` is at the head, a dynamic cell nearer | From the head to that cell's centre |
| The smallest `clear()` is at a path sample | The direction of travel v / \|v\| |
| Head-frame branch | The sector's centre azimuth, already in head space |

World to head space: with h = the head's forward axis projected on the floor (the head's up axis stands in when
the head looks straight up or down), right = (h.y, −h.x): `Direction = (dot(w, right), dot(w, h))`, normalised.

### 8.7 Rescan advised

On every processed depth image, over the samples whose hit lies within 0.5 m of the edge of FREE
(|`sdf.Sample(P.xz)`| < 0.5) and between 1.0 m and 2.0 m above the floor (walls, not furniture):
`e = |sdf.Sample(P.xz) + 0.05|`, which is 0 when the hit lies on a wall face of the static map. With at least 50 such samples, `m` = their median, taken from a histogram of 5 mm bins. `RescanAdvised` becomes true
when m > 0.15 m continuously for 2.0 s, and false when m < 0.10 m continuously for 2.0 s. A gap of more than
0.5 s with no compared image restarts either count. It needs tracking Normal, a tracked anchor and an active room. The cue is the "rescan" icon with the WARN haptic; the session is then rescanned
(§13.2 of the plan).

`Gamepad.SetMotorSpeeds`: (0.25, 0.35) for `Warn`, (0.8, 1.0) for `Strong`, off otherwise. On the phone it reports
`Available` false and sends nothing in this Unity version: the Input System has no rumble on iOS before Unity
6000.7 (IN §2.5; open item 9).

### 8.10 What Bump Guard never does

No registry parameter, no off switch, no read of the link, no file, no `await`. It keeps working with the Station
unplugged (rule 1 of §0.5). `Safety_Independence` checks the assembly graph from G0 and the pose jump from G5.

Rules:

- **Who calls what.** `Build` runs on `ISpaceHost.RoomChanged` (§14.2). A space sets `SetWallMode`,
  `FloorVisible`, `ObjectsVisible` and its warp properties on `ShellMaterial` and on each view's `Material` in
  `Enter`. SpaceLab calls `ResetPresentation()` between two spaces. The asset client calls `SetObjectState`,
  `SetGeneratedMesh` and `SetBuiltInMesh`. Nobody else changes the room.
- **State changes.** `Data.Mesh`, `Data.AssetHash` and `ObjectChanged` are set when a mesh swap begins, not when
  its fade ends. `SetObjectState` is refused (false) once an object shows a generated, built-in or stand-in
  mesh, and while a swap of it is queued.
- **Saving.** Nothing saves the RoomModel on `ObjectChanged` in this build: a saved room holds the mesh states
  of the moment it was saved (§14.2, row 3), so the `boxId → sha256` record of §7.4 is not kept on the phone.
  An object that a loaded room lists as `Ready` or `BuiltIn` is drawn as its box until the asset client places
  the mesh again.
- **Materials.** One pooled instance for the shell and one per object, never a `MaterialPropertyBlock`. A
  curved space writes its per-material anchor to these instances: the shell uses the room's coordinates as
  tile-zero coordinates; each object gets its own anchor at its scanned position so it stays rigid (§4.6).
- **Bounds.** A space that warps the room sets large `localBounds` on `FloorRenderer`, `WallRenderer` and each
  view's `Renderer` itself, and restores them in `Exit`.
- **Cross-fade.** By dither, with the alpha of the material's base tint (`_BaseTint.a`, lane A §10.8): the old
  mesh fades out on its own rented material while the new one fades in. One extra draw call and one extra pool
  material per object for 0.3 s.
- **At most one mesh swap begins per frame**; further calls in the same frame queue (worst frame < 20 ms, §7.4).
- **Ticking.** Wall changes and cross-fades advance in `RoomContent.Tick`, which the installer hooks to the frame
  loop's upload step after boot. With no `IFrameLoop` published the content stays `Instant`: no sinking, no fade.

### 9.3 The Home shell

Generated from the RoomModel by `RoomShellBuilder` (engine-free) into lane A's `AtlasMeshBuilder`
(`Rendering/Look/AtlasMeshBuilder.cs`), then turned into a `Mesh` with `AtlasMesh.Create` (lane A §10.2).

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Floor | The `outline` polygon at y = 0, facing up (`RoomFloorMesher`). Cut by the grid lines x = 0.25·i and z = 0.25·j of the room frame (a line passes through the origin): each grid cell is clipped to the outline and fanned about its centroid. A cell wholly inside is four triangles, so no floor edge is over 0.25 m ("strict"). No holes under furniture. `Floor(WalkablePolygon, …)` cuts the same floor from a polygon with holes, for a space that draws only where the walker may go |
| Walls | One strip per `walls[k]`, facing the room (the left of a → b), one-sided. Along the wall: cut at 0, at both edges of every opening and at the end; each span is divided into `ceil(span / 0.25)` equal columns. Upwards: a span with no opening is one panel from 0 to `height`; a span inside an opening has a panel below `bottom` (when `bottom` > 0) and one above `top` (when `top` < `height`). Each panel is divided into `ceil(panel height / band)` equal rows, band = 0.25 m. No jambs, sills or wall thickness |
| Budget | Floor + walls ≤ **3,000 triangles** (§8.4). When the count would be higher, detail is lowered in this order: the wall `band` doubles (0.5, 1, 2, 4 m); then the floor's cells become two triangles each (diagonal 0.354 m); then the floor's pitch doubles (0.5, 1, 2, 4 m). Vertical edges may be long because the warp leaves height untouched (§4.6). A 5 × 5 m room: 1,600 (strict) with band 0.5 m. A 6 × 8 m room: 1,536 + 1,120, two triangles per cell, band 0.5 m. A 9 × 9 m room, the largest RoomPlan handles: 2,592 + 288, band 4 m. A 14 × 14 m room: pitch 0.5 m |
| Edges | The floor has no edge over 0.25 m at full detail. Walls and kerbs have no edge whose length along the floor is over 0.25 m (`RoomShellBuilder.LongestFloorPlaneEdge`); a wall cell's diagonal in space is 0.354 m and its rows may be taller (decided here) |
| Kerbs | One strip per wall, `KerbHeight` high and 0.10 m thick, outwards from the wall line: inner face, top, outer face; columns of at most 0.2291 m (`KerbColumn`), so the diagonal of a cell of the kerb's top is 0.25 m; one row. Spans under a `door` or `opening` are left out; windows are ignored. Drawn instead of the walls: 6 triangles per column. A post stands at every wall corner: radius 0.06 m, height 0.9 m, 8 sides, palette cell `Post` (21) (plan §4.6). On `budget_room`: 77 columns and four posts of 72 triangles, 750 in all |
| Sinking | `SetWallMode(Kerbs, t)`: the full walls scale down in Y to `KerbHeight / height` over t, then the kerb mesh replaces them. `Full` reverses it. `Hidden`: the walls scale to zero and are disabled |
| Renderers | Two: floor and walls (or kerbs), sharing `ShellMaterial`. Two draw calls per eye |
| Vertex data | `AtlasVertex` (lane A §10.2). Position in the room frame. UV0: the palette coordinate of `Floor` (16), `Wall` (18), `Kerb` (20) or `Post` (21). Shade: `AtlasShading.FaceShade(normal)` times an ambient occlusion of 1.0 that falls to 0.6 over the lowest 0.25 m of a wall and the outermost 0.25 m of the floor (decided here). UV1 and line weight: on the floor (x, z) ÷ 0.5 with weight 255, which draws the 0.5 m floor grid of §8.1; on walls and kerbs zero |
| Accuracy | Vertices on the outline are the outline's own coordinates, so the virtual wall is the scanned wall (G5: within 0.05 m of the real one at three touch points) |

### 9.5 Stand-in meshes: exactly 5,000 triangles

`budget_room` measures the full furniture load without the Forge. A stand-in is the **ellipsoid inscribed in the
object's box** (decided here): a closed surface whose triangle count does not depend on the box.

```text
a = w / 2,  b = h / 2,  c = d / 2          S = 50 segments,  B = 51 bands
top pole     (0, 2b, 0)
ring k = 1..50, segment j = 0..49:   θ = π·k / 51,  λ = 2π·j / 50
             ( a·sin θ·cos λ ,  b + b·cos θ ,  c·sin θ·sin λ )
bottom pole  (0, 0, 0)
triangles:   50 (top fan) + 49 · 50 · 2 (bands) + 50 (bottom fan) = 5,000        vertices: 2 + 50 · 50 = 2,502
normal:      normalise( x / a² , (y − b) / b² , z / c² )          occlusion: 0.6 + 0.4 · y / h
```

The longest edge is about 0.14 times the longest half-size: 0.116 m for the 1.8 m couch, and under 0.25 m for
any box up to 3.5 m. Winding is Unity's (clockwise seen from outside). UV0 is the category's palette coordinate;
shade is `AtlasShading.FaceShade(normal)` times the occlusion; no lines.
`StandInMeshBuilder.Build(float3 size, ObjectCategory category, AtlasMeshBuilder data)` appends it; the test
`StandIn_Exact5000` checks the count, the edge and the table of §5.3.

### 10.1 Routes

| Route | Built now | Needs | Gives | Falls back to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Manual outline | **Yes** | A floor to ray-cast (a horizontal plane), or nothing at all with `AddCornerAtFeet` | The outline. FLOOR = the whole outline, no furniture. With fewer than three corners the outline is the walked path grown by 0.30 m and squared up (`OutlineBuilder.FromPath`) | Always available |
| 2 FloorScan | **Yes** | LiDAR mesh with face classes, planes | A proposed outline (the edge of what was seen, simplified to 0.08 m and squared up), free floor with holes for furniture, wall directions, obstacle boxes | Route 1 when there is no LiDAR mesh or the mesh gives no walkable floor. | 3 Object boxes | **Yes** | RoomPlan through `ARBoundingBoxManager` (iOS 17, LiDAR) | A category and a clean box per piece of furniture, merged with route 2's boxes (rule G13; `ObjectBoxMerge`). A box that leaves no walkable floor is dropped | Route 2 (objects stay `unknown`) |
| 4 Wall bridge | **No** (Extended, `P1-C05`) | A Swift plugin | Clean walls, doors, windows | `ScanRoute.WallBridge` is reserved. Until then `walls` are the outline's edges at 2.4 m, or at the height of the wall planes seen, with no openings |

Each later route adds to the earlier ones. `quality.route` is the highest that contributed. The routes are one
calculation, `ScanSolver.Solve(ScanSnapshot scan, string roomId, float defaultWallHeight, bool buildRoom)`,
engine-free, run on a worker thread. Where meshes carry no face classes (XR Simulation, a phone with no LiDAR)
the level planes are laid out as triangles and go through the same grid pipeline; that room is reported as
route 1, or route 3 with boxes. The scan's own obstacle boxes are fed back to FloorScan as blocking boxes, so no
free floor lies under a placeholder box (R18).

### 10.2 States

```text
Idle ──Begin──> Starting ──session tracking──> Sweeping ──EndSweep──> Processing ──> AwaitingConfirm
                                                                           ^               │   │
                                                              SetOutline / MoveCorner ─────┘   │ Confirm
                                                                                               v
                                    Failed <── (no floor, too small, tracking lost 10 s)   Finalising ──> Done
Cancel: any state ──> Idle (Failure reads Cancelled).   Begin is accepted in Idle, Done and Failed.
UseRoom / UseFixture: any state ──> Done with no scan (ResultAnchor is null).
```

| State | What happens | Leaves when |
|---|---|---|
| `Starting` | Scan features on (§10.5); key-frame ring cleared. The caller has already left the headset view, which disarms Bump Guard (§14.2) | The session tracks (`Normal`) |
| `Sweeping` | Mesh, planes, boxes and key frames collect. The walker's floor path is recorded. A preview outline is recomputed off the main thread twice a second. Corners may be tapped (route 1) | `EndSweep()` |
| `Processing` | `ScanSolver.Solve` (`FloorScanCore.Run`, then `RoomModelBuilder.Build`) on a worker thread (§2.8), on the snapshot taken at `EndSweep` | Result ready |
| `AwaitingConfirm` | `Proposal` is valid: the walker sees the outline, the free rectangle and the lap square on the floor | `Confirm()`, a correction, or `Cancel()` |
| `Finalising` | Scan features off at once. On the session's next `Tick` the anchor is created at the free rectangle's centre: an `ARAnchor` component added to an object "Atlas Room Anchor" under `XROrigin.TrackablesParent`, which registers synchronously (not `TryAddAnchorAsync`). The RoomModel is completed (anchor id, time) | That `Tick` |
| `Done` | `Result` and `ResultAnchor` are valid. After a confirmation, photo selection and cropping start (§11) | `Begin` |
| `Failed` | `Failure` says why. Scan features off | `Begin` |

`Failed` carries a reason: `NoFloor` (`EndSweep` with no floor in the mesh, fewer than three corners and no walked
path), `TooSmall` (§2.8), `TrackingLost` (tracking not `Normal` for 10 s during the sweep,
`ScanFlow.TrackingLostSeconds`; decided here). When the anchor cannot be created the room keeps its saved pose and
`ResultAnchor` is null. With no plane, tap height or mesh, the floor height is taken as 0 in the session frame. Budget:
`EndSweep` to `AwaitingConfirm` in well under a second of computing; scan end to walkable Home ≤ 90 s (§11.2).

Outline rules (`OutlineBuilder`): a corrected outline is made counter-clockwise, must be simple and have no edge
under 0.05 m. An invalid correction is refused: the state stays `AwaitingConfirm`, the proposal is unchanged, and
`Failure` reads `OutlineInvalid` until the next valid correction. A valid correction goes through `Processing`
again, on the snapshot taken at `EndSweep`; one that yields no room keeps the old proposal and sets `Failure`.
Tapped corners are squared up when within 15° of a right angle. With taps and a mesh,
the tapped outline wins: the walker's outline is what FREE is intersected with (rule G7). Glass doors and mirrors
are left outside the outline by the walker (§4.10).

### 10.4 Onboarding

`Atlas.App.Onboarding.OnboardingFlow` (lane C's files in `Atlas.App`). Phone in hand until the last step. The flow

### 10.5 AR session configuration

## 11 · Object photo capture

Plan §4.5 and §7.4, task `P1-C08`. `Atlas.Scan.ObjectPhotoCapture`, the thin engine side of the engine-free
`PhotoCaptureCore`. Nothing is written to disk; only crops leave the phone. The scan session drives it through
`ScanPhotoDriver`: the ring is emptied at `Starting`, key frames are taken only while `Sweeping`, selection and
cropping start at the `Done` that follows a confirmation, and a scan that ends any other way drops its key
frames. Nobody else calls it.

### 11.1 The key-frame ring (during the sweep)

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| A new key frame when | The camera has moved ≥ 0.25 m **or** turned ≥ 15° since the last key frame, **and** its turn rate is below 30°/s (from the last two raw rotations) |
| Capacity | 96 frames; when full, the oldest is dropped |
| A key frame holds | The camera image (`TryAcquireLatestCpuImage`; on ARKit the two planes of `IosYpCbCr420_8BiPlanarFullRange`, copied; the `XRCpuImage` is disposed at once, AF §7.3), as delivered up to 1280 pixels wide and with every n-th pixel kept above that; its size; the tracked camera's pose in the AR session frame (relative to `XROrigin.TrackablesParent`, so a moved XR Origin cannot misplace it); the intrinsics; the depth image of the same frame as 16-bit millimetres; and its sharpness |
| Sharpness | Variance of the 4-neighbour Laplacian of the luma plane reduced to 160 pixels wide, computed on a worker thread |
| Memory | About 1.5 MB per frame at 1280 × 720 with its depth image: up to 145 MB during the sweep. Released when cropping ends or the scan is cancelled |
| Cost on the frame path | One copy per key frame, < 2 ms; nothing on other frames |

### 11.2 Selection (when the RoomModel is final)

For each object with `triTarget` > 0, largest volume first:

```text
candidate frame:  all eight box corners project inside the image with a 5 % margin
                  and the projected bounding box covers 10 % to 70 % of the image
                  and the camera is 0.6 m to 3.0 m from the box centre
                  and the stored depth at the projected box centre is within 0.15 m of the
                      distance from the camera to the nearest face of the box along that ray (not occluded)
score = 0.4 · min(area / 0.35, 1) + 0.3 · centredness + 0.3 · min(sharpness / median sharpness of the ring, 1)
        area         = projected bounding box ÷ image, 0..1
        centredness  = 1 − (distance of the projected box centre from the image centre ÷ half the image diagonal)
view 0 = the best candidate
views 1, 2 = the next best whose azimuth about the box differs by ≥ 30° from every view already taken
no candidate: the object is reported with ReportNoPhoto and takes its built-in mesh at once
```

A frame with no depth image, or with no return at the projected box centre, is not rejected by the occlusion
test, so a phone without LiDAR still gets photos. The objects are placed with `room.Anchor.Position` and
`room.Anchor.Yaw`, the room frame's pose in the session frame the key frames were taken in.

Azimuth of a view: the direction from the box centre to the camera in the box's local frame,
`atan2(local x, local z)`, in degrees.

### 11.3 Crops

- Crop rectangle: the projected bounding box grown by 12 % on every side, made square about its centre, then
  cut to the image, so a crop at the image's edge can be non-square.
- Scaled down to at most 768 × 768 (never up), converted to RGBA, encoded as JPEG quality 85 with
  `ImageConversion.EncodeArrayToJPG` on a worker thread (it is thread-safe, IN §7). Each crop ≤ 200 kB; when it
  is larger, quality drops by 10 until it fits. A crop still too large at quality 15 is dropped; an object with
  no surviving crop is reported with `ReportNoPhoto`.
- A crop is handed to the sink on the main thread, one object per frame at most, at step 8b of the frame
  (`ScanSession.PhotoDelivery`, added to the frame loop by `ScanSystems.Publish`).
- When the last crop is delivered the ring is cleared. No camera frame survives the scan (rule 10 of §0.5).
- Extended, off by default: a crop in which ARKit's human stencil covers more than 5 % of the pixels is discarded
  (§7.4). It needs the human stencil during the scan and is not built now.

```csharp
        public RelocaliseState State;        // Aligned, Failed or Unsupported
        public RelocaliseReason Reason;
        public RoomModel Room;               // the saved room, or null when there is none
        public Transform Anchor;             // the room's anchor when Aligned; null: the room stands on its saved pose
        public bool AnchorRestored;          // true: ARKit restored the saved anchor; false: re-created from the saved pose
        public float Seconds;                // from the call to the end
        /// <summary>The room can be activated: Aligned, or a platform with no world map at all (the Editor, XR
        /// Simulation), where the room is used with an identity anchor. False: offer a new scan.</summary>
        public bool RoomUsable;
    }

    public sealed class WorldMapStore
    {
        public const float RelocaliseTimeout = Relocaliser.Timeout;   // 15 seconds
        public WorldMapStore(RoomStore rooms, ArSessionRig rig);
        public bool Supported { get; }                                // an ARKit device with a running AR session
        public WorldMappingQuality Mapping { get; }                   // save when Mapped
        public RelocaliseState State { get; }
        public RelocaliseReason Reason { get; }
        public float SecondsRelocalising { get; }
        public float Progress { get; }                                // 0 to 1 towards the time-out
        public bool Saving { get; }
        public bool Busy { get; }                                     // a save or a relocalisation runs: Tick must be called

        /// <summary>Saves the session's world map as room_id.worldmap. done(false) when unsupported, when ARKit
        /// has no map to give, or on a file error. Waits up to 5 s for Mapping to reach Mapped, then asks anyway.</summary>
        public void BeginSave(string roomId, Action<bool> done);
        /// <summary>Launch: picks the room to return to (the remembered one, else the newest), loads it and
        /// relocalises. With no saved room the result is Unsupported with reason NoSavedRoom.</summary>
        public void BeginRelocaliseLast(Action<RelocaliseResult> done);
        /// <summary>Loads room_id.worldmap, applies it and waits for the room's anchor.</summary>
        public void BeginRelocalise(RoomModel room, Action<RelocaliseResult> done);
        public void Tick(float deltaTime);                            // every frame while Busy, by whoever began
        /// <summary>Drops a relocalisation and a save in progress; no callback is made for either.</summary>
        public void Cancel();
    }

```

Relocalising, step by step:

1. `Loading`: read the file on a worker thread. `Supported` false (the Editor, XR Simulation): `Unsupported`
   with reason `NotSupported` at once, `RoomUsable` true and the room's anchor reset to the identity, so the
   same flow can be rehearsed with no phone. No file on a device: `Unsupported` with reason `NoWorldMap`,
   `RoomUsable` false. A file ARKit refuses: `Failed`, `BadWorldMap`.
2. `Relocalising`: `IArWorldMapPlatform.ApplyWorldMap`. Tracking goes `Limited` (reason Relocalizing) and returns
   to `Normal` when ARKit recognises the room. `Normal` counts only after tracking has been seen to drop once:
   a session that was already tracking says nothing about the room.
3. Once tracking is `Normal`: look for the anchor whose trackable id is `room.Anchor.Id`. Found and tracked:
   `Aligned`, `AnchorRestored = true`. Not found after 1.0 s of `Normal` tracking: a new anchor is created at
   `room.Anchor.Position` and `.Yaw` (a relocalised session uses the map's own origin, so the saved pose holds):
   `Aligned`, `AnchorRestored = false`, and `room.Anchor.Id` is updated. If that anchor cannot be created the
   result is still `Aligned` with `Anchor` null, and the pose pipeline uses the room's saved pose.
4. Not aligned after 15 s: `Failed`, `TimedOut`. The applied map is taken out of the session
   (`IArWorldMapPlatform.ClearWorldMap`) so that tracking starts afresh. Onboarding then offers a new scan.

The decisions are the engine-free `Relocaliser`; `RoomIndex` picks the most recent room and reads and writes
the world map file.

After `Aligned` the room is activated exactly like a freshly scanned one (§14.2). Acceptance: aligned to ≤ 5 cm
within 10 s in the room it was scanned in (G5).

Privacy (§13.5): nothing of a self-scan by a walker from outside the team is written to the phone. Onboarding
calls `RoomStore.Save` and `BeginSave` only when the operator has marked the session as a team pre-scan
(parameter `scan.teamPrescan`, Bool, default off, group `scan`); any other walker's room stays in memory and
is gone when the app closes. `scan.route` (Int, 1 to 4, default 3) sets the highest route a scan tries: 1 the
manual outline only, 2 the mesh, 3 and above the mesh and boxes (`ScanSettings.Options`).

### 13.2 Gaze dwell

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Gaze ray | From the head position along the head's forward axis: the centre of the view, the same for both eyes |
| A target is under the gaze | When the angle between the ray and the direction to its centre is below max(atan(radius ÷ distance), 2.5°), and it is no farther than its `MaxDistance` |
| Several under the gaze | A target the ray really passes through beats one that is only inside the angle tolerance; among those the nearest along the ray wins. Among tolerance-only hits the one with the smallest angle. `LookDown` targets come last |
| Target shapes | A sphere (`Radius`), or an oriented rectangle (`GazeShape.Rect`: `Rotation`, `HalfSize`) for a sign or a menu row |
| Dwell time | `IAtlasInput.DwellSeconds`: 0.7 s by default; the setter clamps to 0.6 to 0.8 s (`P1-C10`). Parameter `input.dwell` (Float, 0.6 to 0.8, default 0.7, group `input`) |
| Grace | Leaving the target for less than 0.15 s does not reset the dwell; another target looked at meanwhile takes the focus only after the grace |
| `GazeMode.Once` | Fires once at the dwell time. Arms again after the gaze has been off the target for 0.3 s |
| `GazeMode.Repeat` | Fires at the dwell time, then every `RepeatSeconds` (default 0.4 s) while the gaze stays |
| `GazeMode.Hold` | From the dwell time until the gaze leaves: `Held(action)` is true, or the target's axis value is applied. A `Hold` target with an action also fires it once when the hold begins |
| Lever | A tap while a target is under the gaze fires that target at once, without waiting. With no target it fires `Confirm` |
| Reticle | A dot 2.0 m ahead under `IStereoRig.Hud`, with a ring that fills clockwise as the dwell runs. It draws with its own unlit shader, `Atlas/Reticle` (`Input/Resources/AtlasReticle.shader`), in the colour of palette cell `Reticle` (204); the material passed to `Install` stands in only when that shader is missing (differs from README F7: `Atlas.Input` references no rendering assembly). Hidden in `InHand` |
| Cost | No allocation per frame; at most 64 targets |

### 13.4 The lever

The headset's lever touches the screen. The service treats a touch that **begins**
(`Touchscreen.current.primaryTouch.press.wasPressedThisFrame`, IN §2.6) as the lever when the context is `Space`
or `Menu`; the stricter `tap` control is not used, because a held lever or a moving head would miss it. In
`InHand` the same touch is an ordinary tap with a position (`TryGetTap`) and fires no action. In the Editor a
left mouse click stands in for the touch (`InputTwins.LeverEditorPath`).

### 13.5 Rumble

`SetRumble` calls `Gamepad.current.SetMotorSpeeds`. On iOS the Input System has no rumble before Unity 6000.7
(IN §2.5), so the gamepad cannot be Bump Guard's second haptic channel on the phone in this build (open item 9).
Bump Guard's rumble does not go through `SetRumble`: `Atlas.Safety.GamepadHaptics` drives the pad itself (§8.9).

### 14.2 Activating a room

One sequence, whatever the room came from (a scan, a relocalised file, a fixture). Lane C's onboarding code
runs it (`AppRooms.Activate`, `AppHeadset.Enter`) through lane A's `Atlas.App.RoomHandOver` and `RehearsalFlow`,
which run the same rows by themselves when onboarding does not own the flow.

| # | Call | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2 | `RoomHandOver.Activate(RoomModel room, object anchor = null)`: `pipeline.Anchor = anchor` (`PosePipeline`; null for a fixture), then `ISpaceHost.SetRoom(room)` (lane A §4) | The anchor that defines the room frame. Kernel configured with `room.LapSide`; `RoomChanged` raised: the pipeline takes the room (world space is the room frame from the next frame, F4), Bump Guard takes the static map, the room content builds the shell and the placeholder boxes. Event `room.active`. In the first frame whose head is in the room frame the frame loop places the walker |
| 3 | For a team pre-scan only (`scan.teamPrescan`, never a fixture): `rooms.Save(room)`; `rooms.LastRoomId = room.Id`; `worldMaps.BeginSave(room.Id, null)` | Pre-scan path. Any other walker's room is not written (§12) |
| 4 | Nothing to call: the scan session started `photos.BeginCapture(room)` itself when the scan reached `Done` | The sink gets `BeginRoom`, then the crops (§11). Lane D sends `RoomOutline` and posts the room |
| 5 | At the headset prompt, `RehearsalFlow.EnterHeadset()`: `bumpGuard.Armed = true`; `IStereoRig.SetMode(DisplayMode.Headset)`; `input.PushContext(InputContext.Space)`. Then `RoomHandOver.EnterNow()`: `ISpaceHost.RequestSwitch` of the entry space (Home), once the walker has been placed | Headset on, Home rebuilt 1:1, Bump Guard live. Refused (false) on a phone with no Bump Guard. Events `headset.on`, `headset.off` |

For a rescan: `RehearsalFlow.LeaveHeadset()` (`bumpGuard.Armed = false`, `IStereoRig.SetMode(DisplayMode.Hand)`,
`input.PopContext(Space)`), then `IScanSession.Begin`. A room in standing mode (`room.Standing`) goes through the same sequence; spaces read the
flag (lane A §4.4).

`Atlas.Spaces.Home.HomeSpace` (S0) is the smallest module: `Registered` is true; `Configure` keeps the room;
`Enter` leaves the room content in its default mode (walls `Full`, floor and objects shown); `Tick` only
follows the switch for its floor marks; `WriteState` writes zeros for `tileId` and `p[3..7]`. Its parameter
group is `home` (`SpaceIds.ParamGroup`) with one parameter, `home.marks` (Bool, default on): a centre mark (a
ring of 0.11 to 0.15 m with a 0.03 m dot, palette `HomeBeacon`) and, unless the room is in standing mode, the
outline of the lap square (0.03 m wide, palette `LapMarker`), 4 mm above the floor, one draw call. The logic is
the engine-free `HomeSpaceCore`, the mesh `HomeMarks`, the cost `HomeBudget`.

## Open for the lead

1. **Bump Guard at the lap corners (open item 19).** With the 0.5 m lap inset a walker faster than 0.5 m/s gets
   a WARN at every lap corner. The alternative is s = min(Wf, Df) − 0.8 m (0.7 m from the walls): a 3 × 3 m room
   then gives s = 1.6 m and L = 1.51 m. In code this is one constant, `LoopFit.LapInset`, and a run of
   `make_fixtures.py`; every reference value on this page changes with it. Decide before G5.
2. **Gamepad rumble (open item 9).** Verified in the installed Input System: no rumble on iOS before Unity
   6000.7 (IN §2.5). On the phone the only haptic channel is the phone's own engine. Accept, or approve a native
   `GCController` haptics plugin.
3. **Headset lever (open item 10).** This page binds a single tap only: `Confirm`, or the target under the
   gaze. Double and triple clicks stay unbound until the lever's behaviour is known.
4. **Which rectangle is "the largest".** Rule G10 takes the longest short side first, then the area. A pure
   largest-area rule can pick a long strip: in a 4 × 5 m room with a 0.4 × 0.8 m side table in the middle it
   takes a 1.2 × 4.4 m strip (5.28 m²) and so selects standing mode, where rule G10 takes 3.4 × 1.5 m (5.10 m²)
   and a lap of 1.1 m. Confirm the rule.
5. **Home shell in large rooms.** The floor meets "no edge over 0.25 m" in full, with four triangles per cell
   (§9.3), while it fits: a 5 × 5 m room does. From about 6 × 8 m the 3,000-triangle budget forces two
   triangles per cell (diagonal 0.354 m), and beyond 9 × 9 m a 0.5 m pitch. Walls keep 0.25 m along the floor
   and grow taller rows. Confirm, or raise the shell budget for large rooms.
6. **The furnished sample room.** "4 × 5 m with a table in the middle" gives a free rectangle of 1.6 × 3.4 m,
   s = 1.2 m, and every dial position at its comfort floor. That is the rule working as written. If a furnished
   sample with a comfortable lap is wanted for the functional tests, the table should stand off-centre.
7. **Kerb height.** 0.10 m for the room's sunk walls (decided here; settable per space up to 0.9 m). §3.5 gives
   0.9 m for corridor kerbs in generated tiles; a 0.9 m virtual wall that drifts through the walker in Curved
   Walk seemed worse than a low kerb. The corner posts are 0.9 m (plan §4.6).
8. **Haptics plugin.** A small plugin of our own on `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator`, one pulse per call, timed from
   C#. Appendix C.3 keeps apple/unityplugins CoreHaptics, which is not in the project yet (`docs/versions.md`)
   and must be built from source on the Mac. Confirm the lighter route.
9. **Bump Guard is armed by onboarding.** In the hand the cue is silent (`BumpGuard.Armed`), because the phone
   points at furniture all the time during a scan. Nothing remote reaches the flag. Confirm that this is not the
   "off switch" §4.10 forbids.
10. **Depth image size and meaning.** The plan says 256 × 192. The managed sources do not show the size, nor
    whether the value is depth along the camera axis or range (AF §8.4, D4). The types carry width, height and
    intrinsics, so no code depends on it, but the sector and coverage numbers of §4.10 do. First device check.
    Related: with `DepthMin` = 0.3 m the head-frame fallback (§8.5) sees nothing nearer than 0.3 m, so while
    tracking is lost a walker who stands still never gets WARN from depth alone. Decide whether a return below
    the valid range should count as "very near" there.
11. **Camera picture during the scan.** Onboarding in the hand shows the camera picture so the walker can see
    the floor they tap (lane A's `DisplayMode.Hand`). Both pages read D19 as a rule for the headset. Confirm.
12. **Additions to §4.3.** `version`, `id`, `createdUtc`, `outline`, `triTarget`, the `standIn` mesh state and
    the `tiny_standing` fixture are not in the plan's field list. The comfort floors are stored in the room
    (open item 7): after G2 the constants change in `LoopFit` and the fixtures are regenerated.
13. **The furniture record of a saved room.** §7.4 wants `boxId → sha256` kept for the pre-scan path. The code
    saves a team pre-scan once, when it is activated, and never again when a mesh arrives (§9.1), so a
    relocalised room starts from boxes and asks the asset client again. Accept, or have the room saved on
    `ObjectChanged` for a team pre-scan.
14. **Two world map stores.** `ScanSystems` creates and publishes one `WorldMapStore`; onboarding creates and
    uses its own (§14.1). They share the `RoomStore`, and only onboarding's is ever ticked. Decide which one
    stays.
