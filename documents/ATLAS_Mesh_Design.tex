\section{The problem and the outcome}

\textbf{What is seen today.} Scanned objects stand in the right place and have the right size. Their shape is wrong: a chair
is a generic chair that often faces the wrong way, some objects are plain boxes, and only some look right.

\textbf{Why.} Position and size come from RoomPlan's box, which is good. Shape comes from three places that all fall short:

{\small
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{31mm}L{87mm}L{54mm}@{}}
\toprule
\hd{Seen} & \hd{Cause} & \hd{Where}\\
\midrule
\endhead
Every chair is the same blocky chair & The real generator, TripoSG, has never run. What is shown is the built-in set: box
composites of 22 to about 100 triangles, one per category & \cd{station/forge/forge/generators/triposg.py},
\cd{tools/gen_fallback_set.py}, \cd{ATLAS_Whats_Left.md} item 9\\
It faces the wrong way & An object's yaw is the forward axis of RoomPlan's box, and nothing in our code decides which side of
the box is the front. The built-in chair has its back on local $-$Z whatever the real chair does &
\cd{Scan/Routes/ObjectBoxMerge.cs:54}\\
A generated mesh would face the wrong way too & The Forge turns a mesh by comparing its bounds with the box. That tells
0\textdegree{} from 90\textdegree{} only when the footprint is not square, and never 0\textdegree{} from 180\textdegree. The
phone already sends each photo's camera pose (\cd{camToBox}, \cd{azimuthDeg}); the Forge does not read them &
\cd{forge/cleanup.py:236}, \cd{forge/models.py:35}\\
A plain box & No template for \cd{unknown} and \cd{other}; only the 20 largest objects ever leave their box; a template that
splits past 1,500 triangles is dropped & \cd{tools/gen_fallback_set.py}, \cd{forge/budget.py}, \cd{FallbackMeshBuilder.cs:41}\\
One flat colour & Clean-up picks one palette cell per object from the photo's dominant colour & \cd{forge/cleanup.py:502}\\
No doors or windows & The RoomPlan wall bridge is written and switched off & \cd{Scan/Routes/WallBridge.cs:53}\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}}

\begin{panelnote}
\textbf{The outcome.} When this design is built:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=6mm,itemsep=0.7mm,topsep=1mm]
\item Every scanned object has a shape of its own kind the moment the room appears, facing the way the real one faces.
\item Within about a minute per object, that shape is replaced by a mesh generated from the photo of that very object.
\item The mesh sits where the real object is, to the accuracy the scan already has. Accuracy comes first: nothing in this
design moves an object or changes its box.
\item Objects show the colours of their parts, in the palette.
\item A room may hold up to 32 generated meshes and 120,000 furniture triangles, with a lighter copy for the spaces that
cannot afford that.
\item Walls have their real height, doors and windows.
\end{enumerate}
\end{panelnote}

Three things are new and carry most of the design:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The object profile} (section 5.2): a 16 $\times$ 16 grid of heights of the scanned surface inside the object's box.
It is made by casting rays straight down onto the LiDAR mesh. It is tiny, it travels in the RoomModel and in the Forge job,
and it is what decides facing, fits the first shape and orients the generated mesh.
\item \textbf{The fit} (section 5.3): the Forge makes the same profile from the generated mesh and compares the two. The
orientation with the smallest difference wins.
\item \textbf{Two levels} (section 5.4): a full and a light copy of every mesh, so the budget can rise without putting any
space over its limit.
\end{itemize}

\section{The design}

\subsection{Area A \texorpdfstring{\textperiodcentered}{·} TripoSG runs on the Station}

\textbf{Set-up.} PyTorch with the cu128 wheels (builds for Python 3.11 exist from 2.7.0), the TripoSG checkout,
\cd{requirements-triposg.txt}, \cd{requirements-gpu.txt}, \cd{FORGE_TRIPOSG_REPO}. \cd{diso} is built from source, so the
Station needs the Microsoft build tools and the CUDA toolkit with \cd{CUDA_HOME} set; without \cd{CUDA_HOME} it builds
without its CUDA part and reports no error. The installed versions and the TripoSG commit go into \cd{docs/versions.md}.

\textbf{What the Forge's first round established beyond the research above} (\cd{station/forge/README.md}, ``First run on
the Station''; nothing here was run on the Station):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The lock file did not install on Windows.} \cd{requirements.lock} pinned \cd{uvloop} with no platform marker, and
\cd{uvloop} has no Windows build, so \cd{pip install -r requirements.lock} would have failed on the Station. It is now
compiled with \cd{uv pip compile --universal}: \cd{uvloop} is skipped on Windows, \cd{colorama} is added there, and no version
changed.
\item \textbf{\cd{diso} cannot be in a requirements file.} Its build script imports PyTorch and pip builds in an isolated
environment that has none (seen: \cd{ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'torch'}). It is installed by its own command, after
PyTorch: \cd{pip install setuptools wheel}, then \cd{pip install --no-build-isolation diso==0.1.4} with \cd{CUDA_HOME} set.
\cd{opencv-python} and \cd{pymeshlab}, which only TripoSG's scripts use, are left out of the list.
\item \textbf{The pinned versions.} \cd{torch==2.7.1} and \cd{torchvision==0.22.1} from the cu128 index,
\cd{diffusers==0.33.1}, \cd{transformers==4.49.0}, \cd{huggingface-hub==0.30.2}, \cd{scikit-image==0.25.2},
\cd{scipy==1.15.2}, \cd{peft==0.15.2}, \cd{diso==0.1.4}, \cd{rembg[gpu]==2.0.69}, TripoSG commit
\cd{fc5c40990181e2a756c4e0b1c2f4d6b5202faf8c}. The lists were resolved with \cd{uv pip compile} for Python 3.11 on Windows, and
every pin has a Windows wheel except \cd{antlr4-python3-runtime}, which is pure Python.
\item \textbf{What was tried, and where.} In a throw-away Python 3.11 environment on the build Mac, with PyTorch for the
processor, the TripoSG package imported with \cd{diso} replaced by a stub, and \cd{rembg} cut four of TripoSG's example
pictures, which the Forge framed. No weights were downloaded and nothing was generated. The Windows commands of the run-book are
written from reading, not from running.
\item \textbf{What exists to run it.} The adapter's fallback decoder, the \cd{model_index.json} and \cd{diso} checks, the model loaded when the service starts, the run-book, \cd{tools/check_station.py} (checks the install) and \cd{tools/first_run.py} (posts a room and one photo, follows the job, validates the GLB and reads the frame).
\item \textbf{The weights rule.} An interrupted download is resumed. \cd{huggingface_hub} writes each file as \cd{.incomplete} and moves it into place only when whole, and \cd{model_index.json} arrives within seconds, so its presence says nothing about the weights. The adapter fetches a folder that shows a gap (no \cd{model_index.json}, or an \cd{.incomplete} file) before the load, and fetches once more a folder that shows none but does not load. \cd{tools/check_station.py} reports the gap, and a folder under 7 GB, as a note.
\item \textbf{Winding.} \cd{FORGE_TRIPOSG_INSIDE_OUT} (default off): with \cd{1} the adapter reverses every face. \cd{tools/first_run.py} reports whether the faces of a finished job are wound \cd{outwards}, \cd{inwards} or \cd{unclear}, from the sign of the volume they enclose, and the run-book says what to do for each. TripoSG's two decoders are different code and may wind differently, and one flag cannot serve both; that is not known.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The input picture.} The Forge prepares the crop the way the repository's own script does:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=6mm]
\item Matte the crop. Matting becomes required for the \cd{triposg} generator: \cd{GET /health} reports
\cd{generatorReady: false} when no matting model is installed, instead of sending unmatted room photos to the model.
\item Cut the picture to the matte's bounding box, put it on white, and pad it to a square by 10\,\% of its long side on every
side.
\item Hand that picture to the pipeline. The original crop and its matte are kept in memory for the colour step (section 5.3).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The views.} Facing is known when the photos are chosen (section 5.2), so selection can prefer what the model was
trained on: a view's score is halved when the camera stands in the back half of the object, or more than 30\textdegree{}
above its centre. A view from behind is still taken when there is no other.

\textbf{The output frame.} Which way is up, and where the mesh's front is, is measured once on the Station with a test photo of an object that is clearly not symmetric (a chair seen from the front-left); \cd{tools/first_run.py} reads both from a finished job. The answer is set as \cd{triposg_up} and \cd{triposg_front} in \cd{forge/config.py}: settings read from \cd{FORGE_TRIPOSG_UP} and \cd{FORGE_TRIPOSG_FRONT} (defaults \cd{Y} and \cd{unknown}) and checked at start-up. \cd{triposg_up} is the only up axis tried for a TripoSG mesh; if TripoSG's up is another axis, every job fails with \cd{asset contract: [frame.bounds]} until the setting is measured, and a mesh standing on its head is not among the candidates. \cd{triposg_front} is \cd{canonical} (the front is on the mesh's own +Z, which after section 5.2 is also the box's front), \cd{view} (the front is on the side the generating view's camera saw) or \cd{unknown}; it only breaks ties of the fit, and decides alone when a job has no profile (section 5.3).

\textbf{Loading.} The model is loaded when the service starts, not on the first job, so the first object does not pay for it.
\cd{GET /health} reports \cd{generatorReady} only when the load succeeded.

\textbf{Limits.} Generation time-out stays 90 s. The plan's proposal of 60 s per object or less (\cd{P1-D08}) is the figure
check M2 measures.

\subsection{Area B \texorpdfstring{\textperiodcentered}{·} The object profile, facing and first shapes}

\subsubsection{The object profile}

For each object box of a scan that has a real LiDAR mesh (\cd{ScanSnapshot.MeshFromPlanes} is false):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Grid.} 16 $\times$ 16 cells over the box's footprint in the box's local frame. Cell $(i, j)$ covers local $x$ from
$-w/2 + i\,w/16$ and local $z$ from $-d/2 + j\,d/16$. Index $= 16j + i$, row 0 at the lowest $z$, as the room's distance field.
\item \textbf{Rays.} Four rays per cell (a 2 $\times$ 2 pattern at the quarter points), 1,024 per object. Each starts 0.05 m above
the top of the box and points straight down. It is tested against the scan triangles that overlap the box, floor-class
triangles left out. A hit counts when it lies from 0.02 m above the base to the ray's start.
\item \textbf{Value.} The highest hit of the cell's four rays, as a byte:
\cd{q = clamp(floor(255 * height / h + 0.5), 1, 255)}. \cd{0} means no ray of the cell hit anything.
\item \textbf{Coverage.} Cells that are not 0, divided by 256.
\item \textbf{Where it lives.} \cd{objects[k].profile} in the RoomModel: the 256 bytes as base64 (344 characters). The key is
optional. It is absent for route 1, for a phone without LiDAR, for fixtures, and for an object in which no ray hit anything
(coverage 0); a profile is never stored as 256 zeros. It is not part of the geometry hash.
64 objects add about 23 kB to a file whose limit is 300 kB.
\item \textbf{Where it is made.} \cd{Atlas.Scan}, engine-free, inside \cd{ScanSolver.Solve} on its worker thread, which already
holds the mesh, its classes and the boxes.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Facing}

Facing is decided once, when the RoomModel is built, by the first rule in the object's list that gives an answer. After it,
\textbf{local +Z is the front of every object}: the yaw is turned by whole quarter turns, \cd{size.w} and \cd{size.d} are
swapped on an odd turn, and the profile is turned with them. No reader of the RoomModel needs to change.

{\small

\textbf{The \cd{clearance} rule.} The room's distance field is read 0.30 m outside the middle of each side of the box. A side can be the front when the floor there is free and the floor outside the opposite side is blocked, which means the object stands with its back against a wall or another object. A reading within half a cell (0.025 m) of zero counts as neither free nor blocked. The rule answers when exactly one side can be the front and its reading is at least 0.10 m. When two sides can, as in a room corner, the floor cannot tell which is the front: the rule gives no answer and the box keeps its axis. (\cd{ObjectFacing.ClearanceSlack}, \cd{ClearanceProbe}, \cd{ClearanceMargin}.) The rule never compares two readings, so neither the depth of blocked floor nor the proportions of the box can decide anything; a first version that took the larger of two readings turned a toilet, a bed and a refrigerator in a corner the wrong way, with the scanned box right and the file saying \cd{facing: clearance}.

Consequences. The floor gives no answer for an object in a room corner, for one with a neighbour within roughly 0.3 m of exactly one side (a wall and a close neighbour make a corner), or for one standing more than roughly 0.3 m off the wall behind it. \textbf{A corner object whose scanned box is wrong stays wrong}: putting it right would need a rule about proportions per category (the long face for storage, couch, sink and screen; the short face for toilet and bed), which is a new rule and is not built. A wall-mounted screen above a sideboard gets no answer either, because the floor in front of it is blocked by the furniture below; it stays facing the wall as its box says.

\textbf{Two readings} that the table leaves open. In \cd{profile}, ``the next side's'' mean is the highest mean of the other
three strips (a tie gives no answer); the strips share their corner cells, so a scan that saw only the back gives no answer; the
quarter-seen test applies to the highest strip only, so a strip that is highest on a few cells blocks the rule and the next one
answers. In \cd{table}, ``within 1.0 m'' is the distance between the two footprints on the floor, 0 when they overlap; of two
tables equally near the nearer centre wins, and a table that is the object itself gives no answer. In both rules ``exceeds by''
means at least.

The rule that answered is written to \cd{objects[k].facing} (optional key; absent means \cd{box}). Check M1 counts it.

Facing can be switched off by a scan option, \cd{ScanOptions.DecideFacing} (true in \cd{ScanOptions.Default}). With it off the
profile is still computed, because the first shapes and the Forge read it, and only the turning and \cd{facing} are left out. \cd{ScanFlow.Start} forwards the option to both \cd{ScanSolver.Solve} calls, so setting it false switches facing off in the app
too (tests in \cd{ScanObjectShapeTests}).

\textbf{A row of touching boxes.} FloorScan counted an obstacle blob as explained only when one supplied box covered at least half of it, so three or more touching boxes (an oven, a cupboard and a refrigerator side by side) produced an unknown box round the whole row: the largest object, first in the Forge's queue, drawn over the real three. That was a defect on \cd{main}, not of this work. A blob is now explained when at least half of its cells lie under the supplied boxes taken together, each grown by one cell (rule G13); something the provider did not report, standing against a supplied box and larger than it, is still an object.

\textbf{A half turn is stored so that the room reads back.} Pi rounds to 3.141593 at six decimals, past the reader's bound of 3.1415928 and the schema's. Every yaw the builder writes goes through \cd{RoomModelBuilder.StoredYaw}, which clamps it to [$-$3.141592, 3.141592]; a half turn from yaw 0 is stored as $-$3.141592, the same direction. Rule R16 refuses a yaw outside the readable range, so a room that validates reads back.

\textbf{A room saved by an earlier build} gets today's targets on load: \cd{RoomStore.TryLoad} calls \cd{RoomStore.RefreshTargets}, which assigns the current triangle rule unless the room holds stand-ins. The file is not rewritten; the next save writes the new targets.

\subsection{Area C \texorpdfstring{\textperiodcentered}{·} The Forge fits, colours and levels the mesh}

The job's \cd{meta} gains \cd{profile} (as in the RoomModel) and \cd{triTargetLight}. What follows is built and tested on built shapes (the procedural furniture, boxes, an armchair of three boxes, a sphere of 320,000 triangles) and has never been run on a mesh that TripoSG made; what is unproven is listed in section 10. Clean-up runs in two halves. While the job is \cd{generating}, each generation is welded, reduced to the full target, turned and scaled (\cd{orient}, under the cleaning time-out of 60 s, which was 20 s), and that gives its fit. In \cd{cleaning}, only the attempt that is published is finished (\cd{finish}), so no attempt that is thrown away is coloured or baked. The states stay \cd{matting}, \cd{generating}, \cd{cleaning}, \cd{ready}. In order:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=6mm]
\item Weld, decimate to the full target, drop detached fragments.
\item \textbf{Orient by the fit.}
\item Scale to the box, at most $\pm$15\,\% of stretch per axis.
\item Regrid so no edge exceeds 0.25 m and the count ends at or below the target.
\item \textbf{Colour per face.}
\item Bake face shade $\times$ ambient occlusion.
\item \textbf{Make the light copy} from the full one.
\item Validate both (60 s each), publish both.
\end{enumerate}

Steps 1 to 3 are \cd{orient}, run for every attempt during \cd{generating}; steps 4 to 8 are \cd{finish}, run for the attempt that is published.

\subsubsection{The fit}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Candidates.} The up axes times four quarter turns, kept when the largest stretch one axis needs beyond the uniform scale is at most 15\,\%. For a TripoSG mesh the only up axis is the one in \cd{FORGE_TRIPOSG_UP} (\cd{Y} until measured); for any other generator it is Y, Z and X. When no candidate is kept, the orientation the bounds choose and the same turned by a half turn.
\item \textbf{Score.} For a candidate, the mesh's own profile is made with the same downward rays as section 5.2. The score is the mean of |generated $-$ scanned| over the cells the scan saw, in fractions of the box height; a cell where the mesh has no surface counts as height 0. Lower is better; 0 is a perfect match.
\item \textbf{Choice.} Every candidate whose score is within 0.02 of the lowest (\cd{FIT_TIE}) is a tie, and a tie is broken in this order: the candidate with the preferred front; then the earliest up axis, which is the generator's own; then the lower score. The second rule is an addition to the design: without it a cube on a plinth lay on its side, because that fitted a flat scan better by 0.002. Another up axis has to fit better by 0.02 to be taken. The preferred front comes from \cd{triposg_front}: \cd{canonical} prefers no turn, \cd{view} prefers the turn implied by the camera of the view the mesh was generated from (not view 0's, when the mesh came from view 1 or 2), \cd{unknown} prefers nothing.
\item \textbf{No profile.} A profile that covers under 0.10 of the box (\cd{MIN_FIT_COVERAGE}) counts as no profile for the fit; the job still stores the one the phone sent. With none, there is no score, no candidates and one generation, and \cd{triposg_front} alone decides the turn: none for \cd{canonical}, the camera's for \cd{view}, and with \cd{unknown} the orientation the bounds choose.
\item \textbf{Report.} \cd{fit} (absent when there is no profile), \cd{fitYaw}, \cd{fitUp}, \cd{candidates} and \cd{attempts} are added to the clean-up report and to \cd{JobStatus}. \cd{fitYaw} is the turn as a yaw in the box's frame, (360 $-$ the quarter turns of clean-up) mod 360.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Attempts (decision M7)}

A fit above \textbf{0.20} (\cd{FORGE_POOR_FIT}) is poor. With a profile and a poor fit the Forge generates the next view, up to three generations per object, and finishes the attempts in order of fit, lowest first: the first that gives a full copy that meets the contract is published. A job fails only when no attempt does. A mesh is always published (decision M6); a poor fit is reported, never hidden. How failures count: a failed generation is one of the three; after two failures with no mesh the generator is given up for the object, as before, and the hosted service is tried if it is armed; a failure after a poor mesh does not stop the third view; a view that cannot be matted makes no generation and is not a failure; a raw mesh that clean-up cannot use is a failed generation. The hosted service is never sent a second photo for a poor fit: those attempts are made by the Station's own generator only, and the hosted service keeps one generation. With no profile there is one generation, and one retry with view 1 if it fails.

\subsubsection{Colour per face (decision M8)}

\begin{itemize}
\item For each face and each view: the face is seen when it turns towards the camera (cosine above 0.2), its centre projects inside the crop, the matte is solid there, and a ray from the camera to the face centre meets no other triangle first. The crop's own \cd{intrinsics} and \cd{camToBox} give the projection; both are already in the job.
\item \textbf{Colour needs a matte.} A view that is not matted gives no colour, because nothing tells the object from the room; on a machine with no matting model every object keeps one cell, from the dominant colour, as before. A view also needs \cd{intrinsics} and \cd{camToBox}, and a camera that cannot be one is left out. The colours are read from every photo the phone sent, not only from those a generation used.
\item The face's colour is the mean of the 5 $\times$ 5 pixels around the projected centre, weighted by the cosine, over the views that see it.
\item It is mapped to the nearest object cell of the palette (cells 64 to 127), with today's \cd{nearest_entry}.
\item Smoothing: three passes in which a face takes the cell that two of its three neighbours share; then regions under 2\,\% of the surface merge into their largest neighbour, and the smallest regions merge until at most 6 cells remain.
\item A face no view saw takes the cell of the nearest seen face along the surface. With no seen face at all, the object keeps one cell from the dominant colour, as today.
\item Vertices are split only where two faces of different cells meet, and every vertex of a face points at the centre of that face's cell. The shader, the palette and the vertex format do not change. \cd{AssetReady.palette} reports the cell that covers the most surface.
\item The faces must be wound counter-clockwise seen from outside, as the GLB stores them. If a generator winds them the other way, no face is seen and every object takes one cell (section 10).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Four departures in \cd{forge/colour.py}.} Speckle is counted after the three passes and the 2\,\% merge, not after the limit to 6 cells, because counted after the limit a clean object of eight equal stripes limited to three cells looks speckled and collapses to one cell. A face no view saw takes the cell of the nearest seen face by distance along the surface, centre to centre over faces that share a corner or an edge, not by a count of edges crossed, because the count ran the border between two colours at 45\textdegree{} across an unseen back. After the unseen faces are filled, the 2\,\% merge and the limit run a second time over the whole mesh, so a small island of seen faces surrounded by unseen ones cannot survive. And ``no other triangle first'' is an exact ray test from the camera to each face centre, with triangles binned by their footprint in the picture, not a depth buffer (tolerance 0.01 m; a view that would need more than 12,000,000 ray-triangle tests sees nothing).

\subsubsection{Two levels (decision M10)}

\begin{itemize}
\item The full copy meets the object's full target (section 5.4); the light copy meets the room's light target.
\item The light copy is made from the full one by decimation and is then put back on the full copy's bounds (a linear map per axis), so that both fill the same box. When the full copy is already within the light target, the light copy is the same file: \cd{lightSha256} equals \cd{sha256}. If the light copy cannot keep every edge under 0.25 m inside its target, there is none, the report says why, and the full copy is published all the same.
\item \cd{AssetReady} gains \cd{lightSha256}, \cd{lightUrl}, \cd{lightBytes} and \cd{lightTris}, present when there is a light copy; a Forge cache hit answers with the same four. A reader that ignores them shows the full copy everywhere, as today.
\item \cd{objects[k].assetHashLight} records the light copy beside \cd{assetHash}.
\item The phone downloads the full copy first, since the walker is in Home when meshes arrive, then the light one. A transport failure of the light copy (the download after its three attempts, a work time-out, no cache with no Forge) keeps it pending and tries again after 15 s, then 30, 60 and 120 s, and at once when the Forge returns; a content failure (the hash, the validation, the load, the room content refusing it) drops it for good, and the object keeps its full copy. When \cd{lightSha256} equals \cd{sha256} there is no second download. A first shape that a mesh replaces is destroyed only when the room content no longer draws it (\cd{IRoomContent.IsMeshDrawn}).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{The record (decision M14)}

already outside git (\cd{station/forge/.gitignore}), it holds: the RoomModel with its profiles, the crops, the generator's raw
output per attempt, the job reports, the published GLBs, and \cd{truth.json}, written once by hand: for each object, which
side is really the front. Because the raw generator output is kept, clean-up can be changed and run again without the GPU.

\cd{tools/meshcheck/check.py} reads the record and prints the numbers below. Only those numbers, and pictures rendered from
the meshes, go into \cd{docs/evidence/mesh/}. No camera frame leaves the Station.

\textbf{As built.} \cd{python tools/meshcheck/check.py --data DIR --room ID [--truth FILE] [--poor-fit X] [--json FILE] [--pictures DIR]}, with the Forge's interpreter; the truth defaults to \cd{rooms/<roomId>/truth.json} of the data folder. \cd{truth.json} maps each object id to \cd{+Z}, \cd{+X}, \cd{-Z}, \cd{-X} or \cd{none}: the side of the box, as the RoomModel holds it, that is really the front. \subsection{A ray-cast renderer: a future option}

Not built now. This is what it would be and what would have to be true first.

\textbf{What exists.} Curved space is drawn by moving every vertex with the warp, which places it by the geodesic from the
head: its direction and its length. A straight line of sight from the head is therefore already a geodesic. At every vertex
the picture is exactly what a ray caster would draw. The only error is along an edge between two vertices, which is drawn
straight where the true edge bends, and the 0.25 m edge rule bounds it.

\textbf{What a ray caster would bring.} No edge rule, so no triangles spent only on bending: a table top would no longer need
a grid. Exact depth order on the sphere, where the far side wraps round. It would not add detail to any mesh, and it would
not change where anything stands.

\textbf{What it costs.} Every pixel follows a geodesic into the scene, for two eyes, 60 times a second, on a phone. That is
practical for content given by a formula (the floor and the tiling) and for distance fields. For triangle meshes it needs a
search structure in the shader or hardware ray tracing, and whether Unity offers that on the target phones has not been
checked.

\textbf{A path in three steps, each useful alone.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=6mm]
\item \textbf{A reference ray caster in the Editor}, on the processor: the exact picture of a test room. It measures the warp's
error in pixels against edge length and distance. If the error is under a pixel beyond some distance, the edge rule can be
relaxed there, which saves triangles with no renderer at all.
\item \textbf{The floor and the tiles per pixel}, in the fragment shader: the tiling is a formula, so a pixel can find its tile
without a mesh.
\item \textbf{Furniture as distance fields}, made by the Forge as a third copy, traced per pixel.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Before starting.} Check M3 has shown spare frame time; step 1 has shown an error a person can see; and the support for
step 3 on the phones has been checked.

\section{Open points}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=6mm]
\item TripoSG's output frame is inferred, not documented (section 5.1), and is measured on area A's first run. The fit decides the turn, so the front setting only breaks ties; the up axis is not tried, so a wrong \cd{FORGE_TRIPOSG_UP} fails every job until it is measured (section 9).
\item The thresholds are starting values: 0.15 and 0.10 m for facing, 0.20 for a poor fit, 0.30 for coverage, 6 cells per
object. Checks M1 and M2 on the record are where they are tuned.
\item Unity's support for ray tracing on the target phones is unchecked. It matters only for section 6.2, step 3.
\item The two profile implementations differ in one way far below anything measurable. The phone's limits are the float
constants 0.02f and 0.05f widened to double (0.0199999996 and 0.0500000007), and \cd{forge/profile.py} uses 0.02 and 0.05. A hit
within about a nanometre of the 0.02 m limit, or of the ray's start 0.05 m above the top of the box, can count on one side only.
The shared test vectors do not reach it.
\item Three Playground tests fail in the Unity Editor on \cd{main}, for a reason outside this work:
\cd{Blades/Playground/Shell/Cards/HudPanel.cs:117} calls \cd{Destroy} in edit mode. They are not touched by the mesh design and
are listed so that nobody takes them for a failure of it.
\item The fit has only seen clean cases. A mesh scored against a profile of itself gives 0.0 and every wrong turn at least 0.11; other procedural shapes against a chair's profile give 0.15 to 0.46. Not known: the score of a TripoSG mesh against a real LiDAR profile (noise, cells seen in part, the floor seen through a chair's legs, a shape that only resembles the object), nor whether 0.20 separates good from poor and 0.02 is a sensible tie.
\item The bounds filter: candidates must fill the box within 15\,\% of stretch. A real mesh of an object photographed at an angle may fit no way round; then the bounds choose and the fit only chooses between that orientation and its half turn. Tested on one armchair in a box half as deep. The fit is measured after the mesh was reduced to the full target, and a raw TripoSG mesh (its size, inner surfaces, non-manifold parts) was never reduced here.
\item Colour is proven only where the mesh matches the photo and the camera is exact: two colours land on the right faces, also after the fit turned the mesh. Not known: real photos with ARKit's intrinsics and \cd{camToBox}, a mesh that does not line up with its photo (the speckle fallback was tested on noise), rembg's matte of a real room photo, thin parts, and TripoSG's winding (inside out gives one cell per object).
\item The light copy is proven on a sphere (15,000 to 1,875 and to 5,000) and on procedural shapes. Not known: what reducing a real 15,000-triangle chair to 1,875 does to its legs, or how often no light copy results because an edge stays over 0.25 m.
\item Attempts are proven with a generator that returns named shapes. On the Station three generations of one object take three times its time, and with 32 objects of poor fit the queue is long. Not measured.
\item A corner object whose scanned box is wrong stays wrong, and a wall-mounted screen above furniture gets no answer (section 5.2, section 9). Whether M1 on the real record makes a per-category rule about proportions worth building is open.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Formulas}

\textbf{Yaw and the quarter turns.} Yaw $\psi$ turns local +Z towards local +X (lane C contract, section 1). If the front is
found on local +X, the new yaw is $\psi + \pi/2$; on local $-$Z, $\psi + \pi$; on local $-$X, $\psi - \pi/2$; each wrapped to
$(-\pi, \pi]$. On +X and $-$X, \cd{size.w} and \cd{size.d} swap. The profile is turned by the same number of quarter turns so
that its row 15 is the front.

\textbf{Side strips of the profile.} Back strip of the candidate front +Z: rows $j = 0$ to 2. Of +X: columns $i = 0$ to 2. Of
$-$Z: rows 13 to 15. Of $-$X: columns 13 to 15. A strip's mean is over its seen cells, as height $\div$ box height.

\textbf{The fit score.} With $S$ the cells the scan saw, $p$ the scanned profile and $g$ the generated mesh's, both as fractions
of the box height, and $g = 0$ where the mesh has no surface:
\[ \textrm{score} = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{c \in S} |g_c - p_c| . \]

\textbf{The view's yaw.} With \cd{camToBox} the camera-to-box transform of the view the mesh was generated from (view 0, 1 or 2), the camera stands at $c$, its translation.
The azimuth of the view is $\mathrm{atan2}(c_x, c_z)$ in the box's frame; the nearest quarter turn to it is the candidate the
tie-break prefers when \cd{triposg_front} is \cd{view}. Its elevation is the angle of $c - (0, h/2, 0)$ above the horizontal.
